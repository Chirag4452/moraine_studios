MORAINE STUDIO
USER EXPERIENCE CHANGE RECORD
Feature: Product gallery gestures and expanded preview navigation
Date: 9 November 2026
Status: Implemented locally; browser interaction validation pending

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
Enable direct left/right exploration in the product-page gallery and continuous
vertical browsing in the expanded preview. Maintain product context, selected
image feedback, keyboard access, and the existing zoom/close controls.

2. INTERACTION DESIGN
Product-page gallery:
- Native horizontal scrolling with mandatory alignment to each preview.
- Touch swiping and horizontal trackpad scrolling; mouse dragging is supported.
- A small “SWIPE” label with a single right arrow also acts as a next-image button.
- The visual label is 10px; its interactive target is at least 44px high.
- A movement threshold of 8px suppresses enlargement after a drag gesture.
- Thumbnail selection and left/right keyboard commands remain available.
- The selected thumbnail is kept within its own scrollable row.
- Adjacent previews are requested ahead of navigation; previously loaded
  previews remain mounted. Unloaded images show a loading state.

Expanded preview:
- Images are presented in a continuous vertical sequence with stable geometry.
- Previous/next overlay arrows have been removed.
- A restrained “SCROLL TO EXPLORE” cue communicates the navigation model.
- The header, close control and thumbnail rail remain accessible during browsing.
- The active caption and thumbnail track the image nearest the viewport center.
- Thumbnail clicks move to the corresponding image within the preview container.
- The previously selected image is brought into view when the dialog opens.
- Zoom increases image dimensions to 180%; native scrolling supports inspection.

3. MOTION, EASING AND COMFORT
Direct touch/trackpad movement follows native browser scrolling and momentum.
No fixed waiting period is inserted before a selected image is displayed.
Programmatic thumbnail navigation uses browser-native smooth scrolling; its
exact timing and easing depend on browser/platform and are not fixed by the app.
Reduced-motion preferences replace programmatic smooth travel with immediate
positioning. The hint uses a 150ms color transition, disabled for reduced motion.
No bouncing hint, automatic slide advance, or forced vertical scroll snapping is
used. Continuous vertical scrolling lets the visitor control reading speed.
Zoom changes immediately to avoid a large animated scaling effect.

4. EXPECTED EXPERIENCE IMPACT — QUALITATIVE HYPOTHESES
Discoverability: The hint should make horizontal navigation easier to discover.
Its small presentation reduces competition with the wallpaper; some visitors
may still rely on thumbnails. This requires observation, not assumption.
Effort: Repeated gestures and natural scrolling can reduce repeated button
clicks when browsing several designs. Thumbnails remain useful for direct jumps.
Comfort: Native momentum and stable image boxes should reduce discontinuity.
Disabling motion effects for reduced-motion users improves control.
Click behavior: Gallery arrow clicks may decline because users can swipe.
That decline alone would not indicate worse engagement. Preview reach, zoom
usage and product exploration are more useful alongside click counts.
Purchase impact: No conversion improvement is asserted. Purchase intent also
depends on artwork, pricing, download quality, and checkout reliability.
Performance: A vertical sequence can request more image data than a single-image
viewer. Nearby images load eagerly; distant full-size previews load lazily.
Thumbnail requests are small. Actual latency and memory usage require runtime
measurement on representative devices and network conditions.

5. ACCESSIBILITY AND INPUT SAFEGUARDS
Native modal dialog behavior constrains focus to the expanded viewer.
Escape closes it and focus returns to the gallery enlargement control.
Selected thumbnails expose their state through aria-pressed.
Controls have descriptive accessible names and visible keyboard focus.
The scroll region is keyboard focusable. Standard vertical scrolling remains
available, and left/right keys provide direct image navigation.
The single direction arrow is not the only means of reaching a previous image.

6. MEASUREMENT PLAN — NOT YET INSTRUMENTED
Record gallery navigation method (swipe, drag, hint, thumbnail, keyboard),
expanded-view opens/closes, maximum preview index reached, zoom use, and
add-to-cart events. Use anonymous aggregate measurements without raw touch paths.
Compare the share of sessions viewing at least two previews, time to first
navigation, loading failures, and accidental opens immediately after a swipe.
Evaluate add-to-cart rate with traffic volume and uncertainty in view. Avoid
assigning a percentage uplift without a baseline and sufficient observations.

7. ACCEPTANCE REVIEW
Manual review requested on desktop Safari/Chrome and mobile Safari/Chrome:
- Horizontal swipe changes the page image while vertical gestures scroll the page.
- Mouse drag does not accidentally open the enlarged viewer.
- A normal click or keyboard activation still opens the viewer.
- Expanded previews scroll vertically without overlay navigation arrows.
- Active captions/thumbnails follow scrolling and thumbnail jumps work.
- Opening a later image, resizing, zooming and closing preserve useful context.
- Keyboard focus, Escape, and reduced-motion behavior remain functional.
No browser automation is performed without the user's request.

8. ONGOING DOCUMENTATION STANDARD
For subsequent UX feature changes, create or update a dated text record here.
Include scope, exact interactions, motion/easing, accessibility, expected impact,
